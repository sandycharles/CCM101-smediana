## 1. Which cloud platform impressed you the most?

The cloud platform that impressed me the most is **Amazon Web Services (AWS)**. During my research, I learned that AWS provides a very large collection of cloud services that can support different types of businesses and workloads. Some of the services that stood out to me were Amazon EC2 for virtual computing, Amazon S3 for object storage, Amazon VPC for networking, and AWS IAM for identity and access management. I was impressed by how these services can work together to create complete cloud-based solutions. AWS also provides services for databases, artificial intelligence, security, analytics, serverless computing, and application development. Another factor that impressed me was its global infrastructure, which allows organizations to deploy applications in different geographic locations. This is useful for companies that need high availability, scalability, and reliable performance for users around the world. I also found AWS interesting because it can support both small startups and large enterprises. A startup can begin with a small number of resources and gradually increase its usage as the company grows. Larger organizations can use more advanced services for complex workloads. Overall, AWS impressed me because of its flexibility, large service catalog, global infrastructure, and mature ecosystem. My research showed me that AWS is not limited to one type of application or organization. Instead, it provides many tools that allow businesses to build, deploy, secure, and manage different cloud solutions based on their specific requirements and future growth.

## 2. What similarities exist among AWS, Azure, and GCP?

AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform (GCP) have many important similarities because they are all major public cloud computing platforms. First, all three providers offer essential infrastructure services such as virtual machines, object storage, networking, databases, and identity management. For example, AWS provides EC2, S3, VPC, and IAM, while Azure provides Virtual Machines, Blob Storage, Virtual Network, and Microsoft Entra ID. GCP offers similar services through Compute Engine, Cloud Storage, VPC, and Cloud IAM. This means that organizations can usually find equivalent services regardless of which provider they select. Another similarity is that all three cloud platforms operate large global infrastructures with multiple regions and availability zones or zones. This allows companies to deploy applications closer to their customers and improve availability. AWS, Azure, and GCP also provide flexible pricing models where customers generally pay according to the resources and services they use. This can reduce the need for organizations to purchase and maintain expensive physical hardware. In addition, all three providers support modern technologies such as containers, Kubernetes, serverless computing, artificial intelligence, machine learning, and data analytics. They also provide free or introductory usage options that allow students, developers, and beginners to experiment with cloud services. Overall, although AWS, Azure, and GCP have different strengths and product names, their fundamental purpose is similar. They provide scalable, flexible, and accessible computing resources through the internet, allowing organizations to build and manage applications without maintaining all of the physical infrastructure themselves.

## 3. How do business requirements influence cloud platform selection?

Business requirements have a major influence on which cloud platform an organization should choose. During this mission, I learned that there is no single cloud provider that is automatically the best choice for every organization. The decision should be based on factors such as budget, existing technology, workload requirements, scalability, security, performance, and future business goals. For example, Client B, the university, was recommended to use Microsoft Azure because it already uses Windows Server, Microsoft 365, and Active Directory. Azure provides strong integration with Microsoft's existing technologies, which can make migration and identity management easier. In comparison, Client C, the AI research company, was recommended to use Google Cloud because it provides strong artificial intelligence and machine learning capabilities, including Vertex AI and specialized hardware such as TPUs. Client A, the startup company, was recommended to use AWS because of its broad service catalog, flexible pricing, and ability to scale as the company grows. Client D, the global e-commerce company, was also recommended to use AWS because it needs highly available infrastructure, automatic scaling, and global content delivery. These examples showed me that cloud selection should focus on matching the provider's capabilities with the organization's actual needs. A company should not select a platform simply because it is popular or widely used. Instead, decision-makers should evaluate which services provide the most value for their specific situation. Understanding business requirements therefore helps organizations choose a cloud platform that is practical, cost-effective, scalable, and capable of supporting their long-term goals.

## 4. What did you learn from acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant?

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me that recommending a cloud platform requires more than simply knowing the names of different cloud services. I learned that a consultant must first understand the client's business situation, technical requirements, budget, existing infrastructure, and future plans. Each client can have different priorities, so the recommended cloud provider may also be different. For example, a university that already uses Microsoft technologies can benefit more from Azure because of its integration with Microsoft 365, Windows Server, and Microsoft Entra ID. An AI research company, on the other hand, may benefit more from GCP because of its AI and machine learning services and specialized computing resources. I also learned that cost and scalability are important considerations. A startup with a limited budget may need pay-as-you-go services and serverless technologies, while a global company may prioritize availability, performance, and worldwide infrastructure. Another important lesson was that technical recommendations should be supported by clear explanations. A consultant needs to explain why a specific platform or service is appropriate instead of simply giving a recommendation. This mission also helped me understand that different cloud providers have similar services but different strengths and ecosystems. Overall, being a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me to think about technology from the client's perspective. Instead of asking which cloud provider is the best overall, I learned to ask which provider is the best fit for a particular organization, its current situation, its budget, and its future goals.
